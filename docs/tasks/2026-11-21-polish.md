## Sujit: AI (Nemotron)

1. ~~**First real Nemotron call**~~ **Done (1:06 PM).** The default model is now `nvidia/nemotron-3.5-super-vl-120b-a12b`. It reasons before answering, so one photo takes 17–43 s, and the same photo can come back with a different reference object on different runs.
2. ~~**Camera frame reader**~~ **Done.** `python -m flood.read_frame FRAME --camera ID --taken-at ISO [--previous PREV]`. The location is the camera's, from `data/cameras.json`. Super VL compares the frame with the camera's dry baseline and reads the water only against reference objects visible in both. Frame vetting: without a model, a frame unchanged from `--previous` is `frozen_feed` (and the model isn't called) and a very dark frame is `night`; Super VL also reports `night`, `glare`, `rain_on_lens`, `blurry`, `obstructed` and `camera_moved`. `frozen_feed`, `camera_moved` or `obstructed` → `needs_review` with no depth; `night`, `glare`, `rain_on_lens`, `blurry` or no baseline → low confidence, which `predict.py` leaves out. Observations add `camera_id`, `frame_issues`, `frame_checks` and `baseline`, and feed `predict.readings_from_observations` as they are. Checked for real on two public-domain dry Houston street photos (stand-ins, not TranStar frames): both `water_visible: false`, high confidence, 11 s each; with a different scene as the baseline, Super VL flagged `camera_moved`. 12 tests.
   **Prince:** `cameras.json` fields it reads: `id`, `lat`/`lon` (or `location: {lat, lon}`), optional `reference_heights` (same rows as `data/reference_heights.json`, for fixed references like a barrier or sign pole in that view) and `dry_baseline` (already in the catalog; null until a reviewed dry frame exists, then the oldest image in `data/frames/<id>/` is used).

## Pri: Flood math (prediction + router)

The spec for tasks 1 and 2 is [`docs/routing-and-prediction.md`](../routing-and-prediction.md), "The math".

1. **Prediction**, `flood/predict.py`. A Gaussian process per camera over time (add `scikit-learn` to `requirements.txt`). It returns the mean, σ and rate of rise at a given time, never more than 60 min ahead, and None when there are fewer than 4 readings in the last 90 min. *Done when:* the predict tests listed in the spec pass. **Built (1:43 PM, `ed53be3`).**
2. **Least-deep router**, `flood/route.py`: **Built (1:43 PM, `ed53be3`).**
   - Planning depth per segment, as in the spec: camera segments use μ + σ at arrival time (from `predict.py`); estimated segments use the fill's high run; replay uses `max_depth_m`. Unknown segments stay routable but are counted as unobserved.
   - Drop segments deeper than the person-set limit, then try depth caps in steps (0, 5, 10 cm …). The first cap that connects start to end wins, and networkx finds the shortest path under that cap.
   - Return 2–3 options, each with distance, deepest point and unobserved distance.
   *Done when:* toy-graph tests pass, covering: a shallower but longer route beating a deeper short one, no route under the limit returning "no route within your limit", and unobserved distance counted.

## Known gaps (say them, don't hide them)

- The 3D city is today's Houston, not 2017. High-water marks are the **peak** water line.
- The water layer is a bathtub estimate: no drainage, no flow. Beyond the evidence edge is unknown, not dry.
- Nemotron's reading of one photo changed between runs: 3 of 5 runs said wheel hub (0.30–0.36 m), 1 said curb, 1 gave no depth. Every reading still waits for a person. There is no flood-time camera footage.
- The reference table has no row between the ground and the wheel hub, so shallower water comes back as "depth unknown".
- The detector can return a loose box spanning several objects; one of the two close-ups in the test photo was mostly clutter.
- Camera prediction has only been tested on synthetic series. No real camera readings exist yet.
- The frame reader's no-model checks (frozen feed: mean grey-level difference under 1; night: mean grey level under 40, both on 0–255) are untested on real TranStar frames, and the real dry-frame check used public-domain street photos as stand-ins.
- `predict.py` extends the recent trend in a straight line: 15 minutes of rising readings project to about 1.1 m at 60 minutes with about ±6 cm of sigma (synthetic series). Sigma grows ahead, but slowly.
- Inside the 90-minute window, `predict.py`'s sigma does not grow between far-apart readings (checked on flat, step, bump and noisy series), because the time scale can reach 120 min. Routing only looks ahead, so it matters little; `tests/test_depth_model.py` keeps this as an expected failure.
- The replay routes are 85–94% unobserved: the estimate covers little of the road network beyond the high-water marks.
